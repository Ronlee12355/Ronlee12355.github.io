RESEARCH / PATH

Research

From clinical questions to multi-omics mechanisms, and from interpretable models back to clinical decisions. This path runs across cancer and other complex diseases, looking for molecular stratifications that are computable, testable and reusable.

PATH / 01

Research path

A loop that starts at the bedside and returns to it.

01

Clinical questions

Starting from real clinical questions—treatment response, tumour prognosis and resistance—and turning them into computable data problems.

02

Multi-omics & mechanisms

Integrating transcriptomic, single-cell, epigenetic and small-RNA data to reconstruct disease heterogeneity and non-coding RNA regulatory networks.

03

Interpretable models

Using machine learning to turn mechanistic signals into interpretable subtypes, prognostic scores and target priorities, validated in independent cohorts.

04

Tools & translation

Packaging models into databases, R packages and reproducible workflows so results can be searched, reused and carried into clinical research.

PROGRAMS / 02

Research programs

Three interlocking directions: tumour subtyping, non-coding RNA mechanisms, and databases and tools.

01

Cancer molecular subtyping & prognosis

From expression profiles to interpretable clinical strata

Tumour heterogeneity is organised along a small number of interpretable molecular axes. Using machine learning, single-cell data and multi-cohort validation, this direction turns transcriptomes into subtypes and scores linked to survival, chemotherapy or immunotherapy response.

02

Non-coding RNA & disease mechanisms

From regulatory networks to circulating biomarkers

Non-coding RNAs are a key regulatory layer of disease heterogeneity. This direction uses network and multi-omics approaches to identify subtype-specific master regulators, and evaluates circulating miRNAs/piRNAs in clinical cohorts.

Databases & tools

Turning one-off analyses into reproducible community infrastructure

Tools are part of the research. Making data, models and workflows searchable and reusable reduces duplicated effort across the community—and lets methods be tested by real users.
